# Changelog

All notable changes to RoofRoof are documented here.

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- Platform imported from SQMeter 877208b and stripped to what a roof controller needs (spec 001).
- Safety inputs: the weather verdict from SQMeter (HTTP and/or MQTT) and the rain contact, and the park confirmation from N.I.N.A.'s Advanced API and the park switches; logged on change, set through the settings API (spec 004).
- Close on unsafe weather: when the weather isn't safe and the roof isn't closed, the controller asks N.I.N.A. to park, waits for the confirmed park and closes; every step has a time limit, a person's stop is respected, and the critical failures go out as `roof` alerts; new setting `park.timeoutSeconds` (spec 005).
- Roof alerts: seven roof events in Settings → Alerts with their own level, sound and wording: the critical "mount didn't park" and "roof didn't close" (never below urgent, never paused or switched off, sent again until a channel takes them), and a roof fault, a low 12 V supply, a lost weather source, the weather closing the roof and the roof closed afterwards. The `roof` alert event is replaced by `mount_not_parked` and `roof_not_closed` (spec 007).
- ASCOM Alpaca Dome: the roof as a Dome with a shutter (open, close, abort, shutter status, slewing) for N.I.N.A. and other clients, through the same safety checks as the web UI; a client can't move a roof whose position is unknown or clear a fault, and its stop never stands the automatic close down; ConformU runs in CI against the simulator's Dome (spec 006). If Alpaca was already on, imaging apps can open, close and stop the roof as soon as this release is installed (within the safety rules); turn Alpaca off under Settings → Network if you don't want that.
- Data interfaces: the roof's state as one retained MQTT document on `<base>/state` (the same roof object as `/api/status`), Home Assistant discovery of a Roof cover with position, supply, Hall and (with current sense) motor current sensors and a fault sensor, and the automatic close's phase and the weather and park checks in the status. New setting `mqtt.roofCommands` (off by default) accepts OPEN, CLOSE and STOP on `<base>/roof/set`, with an Alpaca client's limits: no move from an unknown position, no fault clearing, no stopping or reversing the automatic close in bad weather, retained messages ignored; every command is answered on `<base>/roof/result` (spec 008).
- The demo has a roof: the firmware's own motion logic, command checks, close-on-unsafe sequence and roof alerts drive a simulated motor, magnets and Hall sensor; the Alpaca Dome is listed; the Demo panel can make the weather unsafe or rainy, unpark the mount, make the park fail, jam the roof and drop the 12 V supply (spec 011).
- Bench mode: a separate firmware build (`pio run -e esp32dev_bench`) for a bare board, with a simulated roof and the motor driver's pins held low, to try the web UI, MQTT and the Alpaca Dome (and ConformU) without a roof. The standard firmware refuses a bench image over the web upload and command-line OTA; a bench board that sees a 12 V supply latches the fault `benchSupply` instead of simulating; the status's new `roof.simulation` field, a banner on every page, the Dome's description and "Bench:" alert titles say so (spec 011).
- The platform while the roof moves: every flash write (settings, restores, saved values, the roof's fault record) counts as busy, so an open waits and a close waits for it; checks and moves are decided under one lock, so they can't overlap; restarts, firmware uploads and WiFi changes wait for the automatic close; the setup hotspot can't open the roof; MQTT settings are applied on the main loop; the start-up log names the reset reason (spec 010).
- Dashboard and settings: a Roof card with the state, the position, Open, Close and Stop (Stop never disabled; Open and Close greyed with the reason, and never offered without live data), the fault with Clear fault, the weather and park checks, the readings and the live Hall reading against its thresholds; a Stop bar on every page while the roof moves; roof faults and a stuck automatic close on the Status card; and a Roof tab in Settings for the motion, Hall, current and supply settings, the weather sources and the mount park, including the park time limit (spec 009).
- Daylight failsafe: a roof left open after sunrise is no longer silent. New setting `daylight.mode` (`off`, `alert` or `close`, default `alert`, so an update never starts closing the roof) and `daylight.sunAltitudeDeg` (default 0, -18 to 30), with the site in `location.set`, `location.latitude` and `location.longitude`; the sun's altitude is worked out on the device. With **Alert** the new events "Roof open in daylight", "Roof closed after daylight" and "Daylight check not working" (`daylight_open`, `daylight_closed`, `daylight_unknown`) say so and nothing moves. With **Alert and close** the roof is parked and closed by spec 005's sequence, with its critical alerts worded for daylight; opening is refused (`daylight`) and an open under way is stopped while the sun is up, a client's stop of that close is refused, and a person's Stop stands it down while the sun is up. Unsafe weather is unchanged: it takes over a daylight close at once (after 30 s of unsafe weather in all when a person stopped it or it was halted). After the update, a device without its site set sends "Daylight check not working: Location not set" once, 10 minutes after the restart. The status has `roof.daylight`, the Roof card a Daylight line and Settings → Roof a Daylight card, and the demo a "Sun up" switch (spec 013).
- Setup wizard: once the device has joined your WiFi, ten pages walk through the essential settings (device name and password, site and time, the hardware with live supply, park switch and rain contact readings, the Hall sensor with its live scale, travel, speed and a 40 % first-run top duty, the weather sources and N.I.N.A. each with a Test, the alert channel with a test, daylight) and end with a review of every check; it saves through the settings API (refused while the roof moves) and never moves the roof. New settings `setup.state` (`new`, `offered`, `done`) and `setup.step`: a new device opens the wizard, a device upgraded with settings already in place gets a dismissible banner instead, and **System → Setup** runs it again. New read-only `GET /api/roof/inputs` (the weather sources, the park and N.I.N.A.'s last reading). The demo restarts it from its panel (spec 015).
- Configurable policies: the automatic close's fixed timings and choices are settings in the new `autoClose` section, each defaulting to the value it had, so nothing changes until you change it: `rearmMinutes` (the safe spell that ends a stand-down, 10, 5-60), `remindMinutes` (critical reminders, 30, 10-120), `notStarted` (`halt` or `retry` for a close that never starts), `remoteStop` (off: an imaging app's or MQTT's Stop of a close in unsafe weather is refused; on: it counts as a person's, with the critical alert), and under Advanced `parkRequestSeconds`, `parkHoldSeconds`, `retryMinutes`, `closeStartSeconds` and `closeGuardSeconds`. Also `mqtt.changeMinSeconds`, `daylight.bandDeg` and `ui.confirmOpenClose` (Open and Close ask first; Stop never does). Each range keeps the safety rules; the hotspot and restart refusals stay fixed. Settings → Roof has an Automatic close card with an Advanced fold and says when Alpaca is off; the docs explain how N.I.N.A. opens and closes the roof over Alpaca (spec 014).
- Calibration run: an attended run that measures the roof instead of a stopwatch. A person at the roof starts it from its own page (Settings → Roof → Calibration run, or the setup wizard's new Calibration run page after the Hall page; the wizard itself still moves nothing); it closes the roof if needed, opens it at the slow duty, asks the person to confirm the roof is now fully open, closes it (and optionally opens and closes it at the top duty), each leg stopping at the first magnet or its time limit (new setting `roof.calibrationLimitSeconds`, 180 s, 30-600) or on the usual faults, and proposes the speed for the travel you measured, the timeout margin, which end reads high and Hall thresholds clear of the in-between noise, each beside the saved value. Nothing is saved until you accept it through the settings save. Refused unless the weather is safe, the mount parked and the automatic close idle; any stop ends it, open and close from anyone are refused (`calibrating`) while it runs, the automatic close always wins, and settings saves, uploads and restarts wait for it. New routes `POST /api/roof/calibration/start`, `/confirm` and `/abort` and `GET /api/roof/calibration`, `roof.calibrating` in the status (the Stop bar shows for the whole run), and the demo runs it. The setup wizard now has eleven pages (spec 016).
- Jog and teach the ends: a **Teach the ends** card at the top of the calibration page sets the Hall sensor's settings from where the magnets really are, so a new roof can be primed before its calibration run. A person at the roof holds **Hold to open** or **Hold to close** to move it at the slow duty; letting go stops it, and the device stops it by itself within 500 ms when the page's keep-alives stop (a closed tab, a lost link), at the first magnet it sees after leaving its start one, and after 30 s with the `timeout` fault; every motion fault and the drive budget apply. Both directions need the weather safe, no daylight close and the mount parked, the park interlock watches every jog, and a jog stops when the weather or daylight turns; open and close are refused (`jogging`) while it runs and the automatic close stops it first. The person marks the closed end, the open end and a place part-way (the reading over a second at rest), and the card proposes which end reads high and both thresholds by the calibration run's rule, saved only on **Confirm and save** through the settings save; the roof at a magnet then reads as that end. New routes `POST /api/roof/jog`, `/api/roof/teach/mark`, `/confirm` and `/discard` and `GET /api/roof/teach`; the demo runs it; the setup wizard now has twelve pages, with **Teach the ends** before **Calibration run** (spec 019).
- Advanced diagnostics: an optional mode (off by default; off, nothing is recorded and nothing changes) with a Diagnostics page at `/diagnostics`, reached from the System page. It shows the roof's live readings (ramp phase, supply at rest and sag, motor current and power, drive budget, time left), the control task's step time and watchdog margin, the main loop's pass time, free memory and stack headroom, and WiFi; a chart of current, supply and duty; and a log of the last 5 moves with profiles and 32 summaries (who asked, result, peak and average current, sag, stall current for a fault), downloadable as CSV or JSON, with **Clear log** (password, refused while the roof moves or a close waits). New settings `diag.enabled`, `diag.keepSummaries` (keeps the summaries across restarts in one small file, written at most every 5 minutes and only at rest) and `diag.mqtt` (`<base>/diag` every 60 s and `<base>/diag/move` per finished move, only while the roof is at rest, not retained), all off, in Settings → Device → Diagnostics. New read-only `GET /api/diag`, `GET /api/diag/moves` and `GET /api/diag/moves/{id}` (`?format=csv`), `POST /api/diag/moves/clear` and the `/ws/diag` stream. Recording runs after the control step has applied the motor output, is bounded and allocates nothing, and no decision reads it. The demo's simulated roof feeds it (spec 017).
- Site data from SQMeter: RoofRoof takes its site from the SQMeter at the same observatory instead of asking twice. New setting `location.source` (`sqmeter` or `manual`): with `sqmeter` the device reads SQMeter's location (a live GPS fix when it has one) from the weather source's address every 10 minutes, never while the roof is busy, and keeps the last good one in its settings (saved only on a change of 0.001° or more), so the daylight failsafe carries on while SQMeter is away; it is never a safety input. A new device follows SQMeter; after the update, a location you typed in stays typed in, unchanged, and a device with no location starts following SQMeter. For a device with no location, that means the daylight failsafe starts working once SQMeter's location arrives: with **Alert and close** it can then park and close the roof after sunrise (never open it); to keep it inactive, set the location source to **Typed in** and leave the location unset. The status has a `location` section, and the dashboard's daylight row and Settings say where the location came from and how old it is. **Copy from SQMeter** in Settings → Roof and on the setup wizard's first Site and time screen lists what SQMeter has that differs (time zone, NTP servers, MQTT broker, SQMeter's base topic for the MQTT weather source, ntfy) to tick, apply and save; no password, token or key is ever copied, and copying a different broker or ntfy server clears the matching saved secret. New route `POST /api/sqmeter/site` (password). The shared settings also keep following SQMeter: new settings `sqmeterSync.time`, `.mqtt` and `.alerts` (`follow`) re-read SQMeter's settings every 10 minutes and take a change to the time zone and NTP servers, the MQTT broker, port, user name and weather topic, or the ntfy server and topic through the normal validation and save (a refused value keeps the old one; the MQTT group never syncs while MQTT roof commands are on). A new device follows in every group; after the update, a group you had already changed stays local. Followed fields are read-only in Settings and the wizard ("Follows SQMeter", with **Edit here instead**); secrets never sync, and a synced server change clears the matching saved secret and asks for it. The status has a `sync` section. A password-protected SQMeter works too: new settings `safety.http.username` and `safety.http.password` (a secret, cleared when the address moves to another scheme, host or port) are sent with every request to SQMeter, and a refused request says "SQMeter wants a password" or "SQMeter refused the password" instead of a bare HTTP status. **SQMeter's address** (`safety.http.url`) is now just its address (`192.168.1.50`, `sqmeter.local`); RoofRoof adds `/api/safety` and SQMeter's other paths itself. A URL ending in one of SQMeter's API paths is taken as its address, so a saved `.../api/safety` URL keeps working (it is shown as the address after the update); another path is refused. A set address is now checked on every save, also with the HTTP source off: one that isn't valid has to be fixed or cleared before the next save. An address that answers but not like SQMeter's `/api/safety` says so instead of "waiting for a first reply", and **Test** next to the address reads SQMeter once and shows safe, unsafe or why not (new route `POST /api/sqmeter/test`, password). `https://` addresses work too, encrypted without a certificate check (a device on your network has a self-signed certificate); the saved SQMeter password is cleared when the scheme, host or port changes. Copying a group also shows its values on their own Settings pages while the channel is off, and each secret SQMeter has set (MQTT password, ntfy token, Pushover keys) is asked for at its field. The demo has a simulated SQMeter with "SQMeter offline" and "SQMeter password" switches (spec 020).
- Test box mode: three owner-set bypasses, each off by default and all ending at one device-stamped deadline, for a real board with a motor and no telescope or weather source yet. New settings `testBox.park` (stands in for N.I.N.A.; refused while a N.I.N.A. address is set, and fitted park switches still count), `testBox.weather` (stands in for a missing weather source; refused while SQMeter HTTP or MQTT is on), `testBox.daylight` (no daylight close and no refusal to open), `testBox.hours` (1-720, default 72) and the read-only `testBox.until`; a bypass or `testBox.hours` sent while one is on stamps a new deadline (renew) and needs the clock set; after the deadline the checks are back, and a backup restore never brings a deadline. No fault, watchdog, over-current, Hall rail, timeout or supply check is bypassed. It shows as a red banner on every page, `roof.testBox` in the status, `/ws/status` and the MQTT state document, "Bypassed (test box)" on the dashboard, a prefix on the Alpaca Dome's description, a Home Assistant "Test box bypass" sensor and the alert "Test box mode on" (`test_box`, urgent by default, sent when a bypass comes into effect and after every restart). Settings → Roof has a Test box card, and the setup wizard's Hardware part asks, after the board, whether a telescope is under the roof. Constitution 3.0.0 words the exception in Principles II and III (spec 022).
- Board presets, pin setup and WT32-ETH01 Ethernet: the roof's pins are a setting (`pins`), chosen from a board preset (**ESP32 DevKit**: RPWM 25, LPWM 26, EN 27, R_IS 34, L_IS 35, supply 36, Hall 39, park 32/33; **WT32-ETH01 + RoofRoof carrier v1**: RPWM IO14, LPWM IO15, EN IO4 with the carrier's 10 kΩ pull-down, R_IS IO35, L_IS IO32, supply IO36, Hall IO39, park IO33/IO5) or set by hand (**Custom**), and checked against the ESP32's pin rules (flash, PSRAM, serial and Ethernet pins, the WT32's header, input-only pins, ADC1, park switches with pull-ups, shared pins, EN off the strapping pins; warnings for the other strapping pins). Pins apply only at a restart, and a change is refused while the roof is busy. A new device has no board chosen and drives nothing until one is saved; at boot it probes for the WT32-ETH01's LAN8720 and preselects the matching preset. A pin set that breaks the rules, or settings that can't be read, drive nothing either and show the fault "Pin setup invalid" (not stored), while the web UI keeps working. A Pins page (`/pins`, from Settings → Roof and the System page) and a **Check wiring** screen in the setup wizard run a wiring check that never moves the roof: the EN pull-down, the Hall sensor, the supply, the park switches, current sense at rest and Ethernet (on a DevKit-set board, a probe that suggests the WT32 preset). New routes `GET /api/pins`, `POST /api/pins/validate`, `POST /api/pins/check` and `GET /api/pins/check`; the status gains `pins`. One firmware image serves both boards: on the WT32-ETH01 it runs wired Ethernet, with mDNS, updates, MQTT, Home Assistant, Alpaca, SQMeter and N.I.N.A. polling, alerts and NTP all working over the cable, and a new setting `network.mode` (`auto`: Ethernet with a saved WiFi network as a fallback after 30 s; `ethernet`: WiFi off and never a setup hotspot; `wifi`: Ethernet not started). Empty WiFi settings are normal there, and in Auto the setup hotspot opens only when no network was joined since boot. The status gains `ethernet`; WiFi scans and joins answer `409` in Ethernet only. A WT32-ETH01's first flash goes through a 3.3 V USB-serial adapter, later updates over the network. Devices upgraded from an earlier release move to the DevKit preset automatically (spec 023).

### Changed

- Bench resilience: a single missed N.I.N.A. reply no longer stops a close part-way with a latched fault. While the roof closes (or runs a calibration leg or jog), a reply that never came, couldn't be read or was an API error lets the move carry on with N.I.N.A.'s last good reading, as long as that reading said parked, agrees with the park switches as they read now, and is at most 10 s old; the roof's control task measures the age itself, so the limit holds however busy the device is, now also for a good reading. N.I.N.A. saying the mount isn't parked, a switch opening, N.I.N.A. not configured or re-addressed, or the safety inputs stalling still stop it at once; starting a close still needs a fresh, good, parked reading. Constitution 2.1.1 words this in Principle II. Settings saves now send only what changed, so a page loaded earlier no longer puts back settings changed since (it had cleared the N.I.N.A. address and reverted the WiFi network on the bench). `GET /api/config` sends the revision in `X-Config-Revision`; a save that sends `baseRevision` and `baseValues` is refused with `409` and `conflicts` when one of its fields (or a credential's partner) changed on the device meanwhile, and Settings offers **Reload**, keeping your other changes. Saves without `baseRevision` work as before. Every settings save, WiFi setup's and a restore's included, now checks and merges under one lock (spec 021).
- Setup wizard redesign: the wizard opens as a dialog over the dashboard (dimmed and blurred behind) and asks one question per screen, about twenty short screens in place of twelve long pages (spec 019's Teach the ends is one of them, still only a link to its attended card): large choice cards for what is fitted or chosen (park switches, current sense, rain contact, Hall sensor, weather source, Alpaca, the alert channel, daylight), a few fields beside the live readings for the rest, a progress bar, Back and Close, a sticky **Continue** that waits for an answer and **Skip for now**, and a **Why this matters** panel. A small dog, RoofRoof's mascot, asks the questions. **Close** or **Escape** asks before leaving (**Keep going**, **Leave for now**, **Skip setup**). The saves, the refusal while the roof moves, the Tests, resume, skip and run again are unchanged, and the wizard still moves nothing; while the roof moves, the Stop bar sits inside the dialog, above everything. The design system gains the onboarding components (constitution 2.1.0) (spec 018).
- The local rain contact is removed: rain comes only from SQMeter (its rain gauge, `reasonFlags` bits 9 and 10, or its MQTT safe topic), which is unchanged. The `safety.rainContact` setting, its reading, `roof.rainContact` in the status, `weather.rainContact` in `GET /api/roof/inputs`, the wizard question, the Settings row and the dashboard row are gone; a saved or restored `safety.rainContact` is dropped without an error, and a save that still sends it is accepted. GPIO4 is free (the WT32 carrier uses its IO4 for EN). Constitution 4.0.0 records it, with the risk it leaves (a gauge that misses rain while SQMeter stays healthy), and words Principle I for configurable pins: the boot reads the saved settings, then drives EN and both PWM low, and an invalid pin set drives nothing (spec 023).
- New logo: RoofRoof's dog, the wizard's mascot, replaces the observatory mark in the web UI header, the browser tab icon, the docs, the app icon, the social cards, banners and stickers. The same dog is used at every size, down to the 16 px tab icon (spec 018).
